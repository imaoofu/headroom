## 2. ⛔ The droop hypothesis — a good objection, tested, and refuted

**GPT's strongest methodological point**, and it was one nobody here had raised:

> The ~13 mV fall is not necessarily evidence that the vendor's programmed V/F mapping is
> non-monotonic. It could be load-line droop or active voltage positioning — a difference between
> requested VID and physical rail voltage.

**It is quantitatively plausible.** On the 2060 Super's falling limb (900→1005 MHz) measured
voltage correlates with a current proxy at **r = −0.891**, implying a **0.56 mΩ** load-line, which
is an entirely realistic figure for a GPU VRM. On the rising limb the slope goes positive, exactly
as droop-plus-programmed-rise would predict.

### The test that settles it

**Droop depends on CURRENT, not on frequency.** Two workloads at the *same locked clock* draw
different power. If the reported voltage tracks power at fixed frequency, it is droop — and it
cannot be frequency, temperature or sweep order, because the clock is identical.

**RTX 2060 Super, same card, same session, gemm vs membw:**

| MHz | ΔPower | ΔVoltage |
|---|---|---|
| 1380 | −23.7 W | **0.0 mV** |
| 1485 | −38.4 W | **0.0 mV** |
| 1590 | **−47.1 W** | **0.0 mV** |

**RTX 3060, copy vs gemm:** up to **+71.2 W** at a matched clock, **0.0 mV** difference, at every
one of nine points below the power cap.

⛔ **The reported voltage does not respond to load current at all.** A 0.56 mΩ load-line would put
tens of millivolts on a 47 W difference. It puts none.

✅ **So the quantity HWiNFO reports on these cards behaves like a REQUESTED voltage (VID) read back
from the V/F table, not a measured rail voltage.** Droop cannot explain the falling limb.

⚠️ **The rows above ~1695 MHz where voltage differs by up to 219 mV are the POWER CAP, not droop** —
`gemm` is clamped at ~168 W and pinned at 0.831 V while `membw` has headroom and climbs to 1.044 V.
Do not read those as load response.

### 🔑 What this does to the finding — it gets STRONGER, and the objection improved it anyway

If the reading is programmed VID rather than a noisy rail measurement, then a fall from 0.644 to
0.631 V is **the curve lookup itself returning a lower voltage at a higher clock.** That is a more
interesting result than a measurement artifact, not a less interesting one.

✅ **And GPT's terminology point is adopted regardless.** "The measured voltage readback was
non-monotonic" is the defensible sentence; "the V/F curve is non-monotonic" asserts something about
the vendor's table that this project cannot see directly. ⚠️ The distinction between requested and
delivered voltage is real and this project already had evidence of it — `CLAUDE.md` records
*"0.925 V requested delivers 0.920 V under ~170 W load — vdroop, not a missing voltage bin"* — and
still failed to consider it here.

**Temperature compensation is now the leading remaining candidate**, which is exactly what §4d's
descending sweep tests. ⛔ **But note what §4d canNOT do:** a descending sweep does not separate
droop from frequency, because current tracks frequency in both directions. It separates
time-and-temperature from frequency. The droop question needed the fixed-frequency test above, and
that test is what actually answered it.

## 3. The 6 mV step — this project overstated it

GPT: *"'Reported voltage is quantized in approximately 6 mV steps' is supported by observation. 'The
voltage sensor has a 6 mV quantum' is not yet established."*

✅ **Correct, and this repository has been writing the second version.** The `rtx2060s-20260912`
README says *"6 mV at a time — one step of this sensor's resolution"*, and `SESSION-E-RUNSHEET.md`
says *"The sensor reports in ~6.25 mV codes."* Neither is established. The step could originate in
VID programming, controller telemetry, driver rounding, or HWiNFO itself.

⚠️ Given §2's finding that the reading is VID-like, **VID granularity is now the more likely
origin than sensor resolution** — which changes nothing about the ambiguity it causes, but changes
what the paper is allowed to say about its cause.

## 4. Turing, and the XBAR chain

**Turing.** GPT found direct clock/voltage observations in the enthusiast press (GamersNexus RTX
2080 FE and Titan RTX; TechPowerUp RTX 2080 Super) and correctly notes these are operating and

verified by reading the paper.** ⚠️ The claim must therefore exclude high-load clock/voltage tables
explicitly, not just "academic work".

**XBAR.** GPT supplies enthusiast reports that voltage-dependent crossbar clocks are publicly
observed on Ada, Ampere and Blackwell — **more sources than the single LACT issue this project had**

and its voltage dependence are all pre-empted.** It agrees the full chain plus a pre-specified
repair was not found. ⚠️ All unread here; leads only.

## 7. The statistics review (prompt 2) — it passed the test, then beat it

**The test:** GPT was given the +465 MHz result with no other context and asked what n honestly is.
The expected answer was **pseudoreplication**. It raised it unprompted and precisely:

> The 12 kernels are not 12 independent replications of the hardware-level causal effect. They all
> share the same chip, firmware, curve edit, sensor, environment, and candidate-frequency grid.

✅ **Correct, and already this project's own position** — TODO item 5 says the same thing. Its
framing is better, though: *experimental unit n=1 chip; independent interventions n=1 treatment
plus 1 control; outcomes nested within.* That is the vocabulary the paper should use.

### 🔑 But its stronger point was one nobody here had made

> The negative control helps, **but only if its edited region was actually exercised** and was
> otherwise comparable. If none of the workloads operated near the control region, "no effect" is
> partly expected and does not exclude another mechanism active only in the treatment region.

**This is a better objection than the council's**, which said the control was weak for sharing
chip, session and operator — the thing a control is *for*. GPT's version is checkable, so it was
checked.

### ✅ The control region WAS exercised, and the edit had a large measurable effect

Peak throughput, control (`repair-suite-p2-20260909`) against stock, at achieved clocks of
**2787–2978 MHz** — well inside the edited region:

| workload | reduce | softmax | layernorm | bgemm32 | bgemm64 | bgemm256 | attention |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| peak change | **+29.6%** | +26.9% | +26.0% | +27.6% | +28.2% | +15.1% | +13.3% |

⛔ **So this is not a "nothing happened anywhere" null.** The edit moved peak throughput by 13–30%
in the region it targeted, and the card demonstrably ran there. That is what makes the optimum
result meaningful rather than vacuous.

### ⚠️ And the check corrected the record: the control is PARTIALLY LEAKY

Measured same-session against `stock-bracket-20260909` (r9), n=12 — the comparison `CLAUDE.md` had
never actually shown:

| | result |
|---|---|
| median optimum, stock → control | **1537 → 1537 MHz, +0** |
| workloads unmoved | **8 of 12** |
| workloads moved | **4** — bgemm32 +145, layernorm +158, gemm +306, reduce +315 MHz |
| **treatment, for contrast** | **12 of 12 moved, uniform +465 MHz** |

⛔ **`CLAUDE.md` said the optimum "moved by nothing", and that is an overstatement.** It is true of
the median and of 8 of 12 workloads. It is not true of all 12, and the treatment claim *is* phrased
per-workload, so the two were not being stated on the same footing. Corrected in place.

✅ **The contrast survives and is still large** — a uniform +465 in 12 of 12 against a median +0
with 4 partial movers. But "clean null" is not available, and a reviewer comparing the two
sentences would have found this.

## 8. The citation question (prompt 3) — ✅ the repository was RIGHT

`verify_citations.py` flagged arXiv 2104.00486: the repo records first author **Wang** and a title
containing "Non-Preemptive"; arXiv leads with **Mei** and omits it. The flag said to open the
published version rather than edit either side.

**Published IEEE TPDS version**, confirmed against publisher-deposited Crossref metadata here,
independently of both arXiv and GPT:

> Qiang Wang, Xinxin Mei, Hai Liu, Yiu-Wing Leung, Zongpeng Li, Xiaowen Chu. *Energy-Aware
> Non-Preemptive Task Scheduling With Deadline Constraint in DVFS-Enabled Heterogeneous Clusters.*
> IEEE TPDS **33**(12):4083–4099, 2022. `10.1109/TPDS.2022.3181096`

✅ **"Wang et al." throughout the paper is correct.** The journal version changed both the lead
author and the title relative to the preprint.

🔑 **The lesson is exactly what the flag was written on.** Had the rule been "make the registry
match arXiv", this checker would have **introduced the project's fifth citation error while
appearing to remove one.** A preprint's metadata is not authoritative for the journal version, and
the registry now carries both deliberately — `firstAuthor` is arXiv's, `publishedAs` is the paper's.
