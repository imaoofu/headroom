## 0. ⚠️ THE SCOPE OF THE CAUSAL CLAIM IS OVERSTATED — fix the sentence before anything else

**No hardware. Highest value thing on this list, and it is free.** From the council session
(`docs/council/council-transcript-20260913-165334.md`), and two reviewers reached it independently.

The manipulation arm is **one chip**. "+465 MHz in 12 of 12 workloads" is twelve *correlated*
measurements on one GB206 under one curve edit — not twelve trials. The four-chip, three-architecture
breadth is real but it is **four separate n=1 studies**, and only the 5060 Ti has a causal arm at all;
the 3060, 3070 Ti and 2060 Super are stock OBSERVATIONS.

⛔ **What must NOT be written:** anything implying the causal result generalises across chips.
✅ **The defensible sentence:** *strong within-chip causal evidence, generalisation uncertain.*
✅ **And the observational cross-chip data is still worth stating — labelled as observational**,
corroborating the correlation, never the causation.

⚠️ **One reviewer's attack on the negative control was WRONG and should not be conceded.** It called
the control weak for sharing chip, session and operator. **That is what a negative control is for.**
Two other reviewers caught this independently: it confuses *narrow scope* (true) with *invalid design*
(false). Do not weaken the control's description — only the generalisation claim.

## 0b. 🔒 The replication the council wanted is BLOCKED, and the reason is this project's own rule

The council's single top recommendation was: repeat the A/B/B/A manipulation on a second RTX 5060 Ti,
on the reasoning that Raymond sells these. **Two things it did not know:**

1. **GPU pricing.** The 16 GB 5060 Ti was bought just before the price hike and that SKU is now

2. 🛑 **The safety invariant forbids it anyway.** *"Never automate tuning on machines Raymond does

✅ **So the replication is OPPORTUNISTIC, not schedulable.** Cards that may realistically pass

5060 Ti 8 GB. **Write the protocol now so that when one appears the experiment runs the same day
rather than needing design** — see item 2b. That is the only way an unpredictable supply becomes
usable evidence.

## 1. 🔌 RTX 2060 Super fine floor sweep — ~10 minutes, and it got MORE valuable today

900–1150 MHz, ~20 MHz apart. Finds where voltage first leaves 0.631 V and narrows the floor extent
from ±60 MHz to ±10.

🔑 **Promoted from 3 to 1 because the search changed what this measures.** Schoonhoven et al. can
only read core voltage on **Ampere**; their one Turing part is in the group where the flat floor is
*assumed* by their Equation 3 and fitted from power, and both ridge points they report are Ampere.
**No Turing voltage-frequency curve has been measured in that literature.** So this sweep is not
just deciding an internal edge case — it is the first measured Turing V/F floor, on the architecture
where the rule appears to break. Ten minutes for the strongest ten minutes available.

Run from inside the kit directory; `tools\Disable-QuickEdit.ps1` first; fresh HWiNFO log.

## 3. Re-weight the contribution sentence — **its opening clause describes a Tuesday**

🔑 **New, from the 09-13 search.** Region-by-region V/F curve reshaping — raise a chosen mV point,
flatten everything above it — has been the standard undervolting recipe since GPU Boost 3.0, is
documented by igor'sLAB and others, and is implemented in public tooling (LACT #936, mVolt+,
PenguinBurner). Read cold by anyone who undervolts a GeForce, *"we reshape the vendor's
voltage-frequency curve region by region"* is not a contribution.

**The load-bearing half is what follows it**: that the optimum tracks the floor region and *not* the
region above it, measured, with a negative control and registered predictions. Nothing found
measures where the optimum LANDS under different reshapes — enthusiast sources measure "same FPS,
less power" at one operating point. ⚠️ Do not rewrite the sentence's *claim*; change its emphasis so
the causal test carries the weight rather than the technique.

## 4. §5.7 must pre-empt the bandwidth-saturation objection

🔑 **New, from the 09-13 search.** A reviewer will say the `membw` plateau is just bandwidth
saturating as core clock rises, which is standard and well documented. **That is a different thing**
— saturation because the memory system is full, versus a plateau because a voltage-pinned
interconnect stopped scaling. The stock control run and the crossbar-to-core ratio collapse
(0.928–0.976 → 0.942 down to 0.726) already distinguish them; §5.7 does not currently say so
explicitly. Cheap, and it closes the most likely reviewer objection to the project's largest body of
original work.

## 12. 🆕 The mutation runner is sequential

`tools/mutation/run_mutants.py` runs each mutant through `subprocess.run` in a plain loop — no
`Pool`, no `concurrent.futures`. Mutation testing is embarrassingly parallel by construction, so
this is idle-core work that would let mutation coverage rise without a slower feedback loop.

⚠️ **Lowest priority on this list and it should stay there.** Nothing is blocked on it, the suite
already runs in ~2 minutes, and CI is not slow. It is here so it is not rediscovered as though new.
