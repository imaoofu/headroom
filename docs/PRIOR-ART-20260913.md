## E′ — the kill, and why it improves the paper

**Wang, Mei, Liu, Leung, Li, Chu, TPDS, [arXiv:2104.00486](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.00486).**
*Read in full; all quotations verified against the PDF in this session.* This is the paper behind
`HKBU-HPML/GPU-DVFS-Job-Schedule`, which is where `scripts/Get-Dataset.ps1` fetches both consumer
CSVs from.

They publish the interval (§5.1.1) — `f^Gc ∈ [0.89, g1(V^Gc)]`, so **the 0.89 is theirs**. They
name the window as the cause of their small saving (§5.2): *"the average energy conservation of 20
benchmarks is 4.3% for GTX 1080Ti... The reason for this low effect is (1) The static power P_G0
takes a big portion in the total power consumption; (2) The scaling intervals of f^Gc and f^Gm are
narrow."* And they run the widened-window counterfactual, reaching **36.4%**, with the optimum
*"close to the allowed lowest setting"* in both cases.

> ⛔ **2026-09-25:** the reading of that optimum as evidence below the window is struck. Their released-parameter model puts the narrow optima near the top of the window; see `CLAUDE.md` (Wang, RESOLVED) and `docs/gpt-findings/2026-09-25-wang-tpds-optimum-discrepancy.md`.

✅ **The paper now cites them for the argument instead of claiming it** — prior art that corroborates
cannot be lost to a paper turning up, so this is a stronger position than the one it replaced.
⚠️ Their energy is **system-scope at the wall** against a 37 W idle floor and their wide case is a
**simulation with static power shrunk**. Never equate their 4.3% with this project's 1.00%.
🔑 **The opening they leave is exactly what this project has: they could only simulate the wide
window.**

⚠️ **Scope note.** Because the GTX 980 files in the sibling repo sweep 400–1500 MHz, this claim is
about the **1080 Ti / 2070 Super / Titan X** files specifically and must never be written as "the
HKBU datasets".

## A — pre-empted on method

**Price, Clark, Barsdell, Babich, Greenhill, [arXiv:1407.8116](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.8116)**
(2014). *Read in full.* ⛔ `PRIOR-ART-20260912.md` calls this "Mei et al.'s K20 firmware study" and
marked it snippet-only. **Wrong authors, and the paper matters more than that log judged.**

They reflashed a K20 with Kepler BIOS Tweaker so core voltages V1–V5 were **fixed to 900 / 912.5 /
925 / 950 / 987.5 mV** — a per-point voltage-table edit, not a global offset. Headline **37–48%
perf/W**.

✅ **The result survives**: under the modified table they hold frequency **constant at 800 MHz** and
sweep temperature. They never sweep frequency under an edited table and locate no interior optimum,
so the optimum is never shown to move. There is no negative control and no preregistration.
⚠️ **But "nobody edits a voltage table per point" was never true and must not be written.**

🔑 **The enthusiast finding is the one that should change the paper's emphasis.** Region-by-region
reshaping — raise a chosen mV point, flatten above it — has been the standard undervolting recipe
since GPU Boost 3.0 (igor'sLAB and others, snippet-level). **The contribution sentence's opening
clause describes something thousands of people do.** The load-bearing half is what follows: that the
optimum tracks the floor region and not the region above it, measured, with a control. Nothing found
measures where the optimum *lands* under different reshapes.

## 4 — the boundary condition got stronger

**Schoonhoven et al., [arXiv:2211.07260](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07260)**, re-read in full here.
Voltage readback *"is only available with fairly recent NVIDIA drivers (510 and newer) in
combination with **Ampere** GPUs"*. Their only Turing part, the Titan RTX, falls in the group that
cannot be read: *"For GPUs that do not support voltage readings, such as the Tesla V100 and Titan
RTX... **We assume** based on our observations that for these GPUs there exists a threshold τf_t
after which the voltage increases with a rate β"* — their Equation 3, a flat floor **by
construction**, fitted from power rather than voltage. Both reported ridge points are Ampere.

🔑 **So no Turing V/F curve has been measured in this literature**, and the 2060 Super result is the
first measured test on that architecture. ⚠️ A piecewise fit also returns a breakpoint whatever the
curve does, so this class of method has no failure mode that reports "no floor here" — *that is our
inference from the models' form, not a statement either paper makes*, and it is labelled as such
wherever it appears.

## D — the chain holds; the date does not

Nobody found reporting undervolting or curve-flattening *causing* a bandwidth regression, on NVIDIA
or AMD. ⚠️ **But the XBAR domain was public two months earlier than `RELATED-WORK.md` §4 records** —
a GIGAZINE report of **2026-06-08** on RTX 5090 vBIOS work, against the LACT issue of 2026-08-10.
**Read as a news report only**; its primary source, an XtremeSystems thread, was not reached and is
the top follow-up. It contains no bandwidth measurement and no voltage coupling, so only the date
moves.

⚠️ **The nearest thing to a refutation was not reachable.** Mining folklore holds that a core clock
set too low costs hashrate because the core feeds the memory subsystem — the right *shape*, but it
concerns lowering the clock, which lowers XBAR too; this project's geometry is the opposite, clock
rising 49% while voltage is pinned. The bitcointalk thread that might isolate it returned
ECONNRESET. **Claim D should not be called clear until someone reads it.**

⚠️ A reviewer will raise bandwidth saturation as the trivial explanation. §5.7 should pre-empt it:
that is bandwidth flattening because the memory system is full, not because a voltage-pinned
interconnect stopped scaling.

## DID NOT REACH — read this before treating anything above as settled

- **The ~95 citing papers of the two HKBU works were judged by TITLE ONLY.** No abstracts, no full
  texts. A range critique buried in a related-work paragraph would not have been caught. **This is
  the largest hole in this pass.**
- **XtremeSystems forum** — the primary source behind the XBAR date. Highest-priority follow-up.
- **bitcointalk 5249082** (Ethash core-clock scaling on RX 470/570) — ECONNRESET. The most likely
  home of a measured core-side bandwidth gating result.
- **Forums natively** — TechPowerUp, Overclock.net, Guru3D, Level1Techs, igor'sLAB Community reached
  only through general search. **Reddit is inaccessible to the agents entirely.**
- **Video** — der8auer, Buildzoid/AHOC, Gamers Nexus. Not searchable with available tools. Buildzoid
  does long-form V/F curve analysis and exists nowhere else.
- **IEEE Xplore, ACM DL, Google Scholar** — still never searched directly. Carried over unchanged
  from the 09-12 log. Mendes/Tomás/Roma, SBAC-PAD 2020 (*Exploiting Non-conventional DVFS on GPUs*)
  is the highest-value unread item: its snippet criticises one-dimensionality in the **voltage**
  axis, adjacent to but distinct from the range critique.
- **Chinese-language venues** — still unsearched, and CLAUDE.md has flagged this twice.
- **Mei's PhD thesis** (HKBU) — not located; a likely home for extended methodology discussion.
- **Patents** — only incidental hits; `US20160093013A1` (the ridge point in patent language) and
  Intel's MEP patents `US11231731` / `US11669114` (closest to `predict_from_curve.py`) returned 503
  and were **never opened**.

🔑 **The residual risk is higher on the enthusiast side than the academic side**, because the two
most likely homes for an undocumented measurement — forums and video — are the two least covered
here.
